Introducing allowances

An allowance is what a duty earns on top of the fare itself: the day worked, hours run past the schedule, a night away from home, a run out to the airport.

This feature gives you nine of them. Each one is recognised and calculated by the system from the duty record your drivers already produce — the booked dates and timings, plus the actual start and close captured on the app. Nobody has to remember which duty earned what.

Every allowance does two jobs. It adds to what the driver is paid that month, and where you have chosen to price it, it adds to what the customer is billed. The rest of this document sets out when each one applies, how it is worked out, and what we need from you to put it in place.

One calculation, two sides

For each allowance the system works out the quantity once — how many days, how many hours, how many nights, how many duties. That single quantity is then priced twice.

What the driver gets

One rate per allowance, set once for the whole company. It applies automatically to every driver, on every duty where the conditions are met. You don't configure it per duty type or per driver.

What the customer pays

Set per duty type, and entirely optional. When you create or edit a duty type, you pick which allowances it charges for and what each one costs. Anything you don't add is simply never billed.

Adding an allowance to a duty type is purely a billing decision. It never changes what the driver is paid — the driver is paid the same whether or not you chose to pass the cost on.

The nine allowances

Each one fires on its own trigger, drawn from the duty record. "Counted in" is the unit the quantity is measured in, and therefore what the rate is a rate of.

How each quantity is counted

The rate you set is multiplied by a quantity, and that quantity is read off the duty record. These are the counting rules in full.

Days count both ends

per day

Every calendar day the duty spans, including the first and the last. A duty running 10 to 12 August is three days, not two. Daily and outstation allowances count this way.

Nights are midnights crossed

per night

That same 10 to 12 August duty is two nights — the driver is away on the night of the 10th and the night of the 11th. Night and outstation overnight allowances count this way, and on an outstation duty both are paid, as set out below.

Hours are the gap, rounded up

per hour

Overtime is the time between the scheduled close and the actual close. Early start is the time between the actual start and the scheduled reporting time. Either is rounded up to the next whole hour, so 10 minutes pays one hour and 61 minutes pays two. Finishing on time or early produces no overtime at all, never a deduction.

Duties are one each — except extra duty

per duty

Off day and airport are flat amounts, paid once on a qualifying duty. Extra duty is the exception worth spelling out: it counts one for every duty after the driver's first that day. A driver running three duties on Tuesday earns two extra duty allowances, not one.

When an allowance doesn't apply

Four situations where the calculation deliberately produces nothing, so nobody is surprised when a duty comes back lighter than expected.

No actual timings were recorded

Overtime and early start compare the schedule against what the driver actually did. If a duty was never started or closed on the app, neither can be worked out and neither appears. Day, night and per-duty allowances still apply, since those come from the booked dates.

The duty was cancelled

A cancelled duty earns nothing on either side and is never billed, however far into the schedule it was called off.

The allowance is switched off

Any of the nine can be turned off for your company outright. It then stops applying to drivers and customers alike, without having to be unpriced on every duty type one by one.

It was never priced for the customer

An allowance you didn't add to the duty type still pays the driver in full. It simply never reaches the invoice — as with night and off day in the first example below.

Six rules we need you to confirm

These are the judgment calls — places where the definitions could reasonably go either way, and money depends on which way they go. Each is written the way we intend to build it, with the alternative noted underneath.

An overnight outstation duty pays both night and outstation overnight

Stacks

A duty that goes out of town and keeps the driver away overnight satisfies both conditions, and pays both allowances. The reasoning: outstation overnight covers lodging and being away from home, night covers the extended hours. They're paying for different things.

The alternative Treat them as mutually exclusive, so an overnight outstation duty pays outstation overnight only, and night allowance fires solely on local duties that cross midnight.

Part hours round up to a full hour

Rounds up

Overtime and early start are hourly. Any part of an hour counts as a whole one — a driver who closes 10 minutes late is paid a full hour of overtime, and the customer is billed a full hour. There is no grace period.

The alternative A grace window before the clock starts, or charging to the exact minute. Exact minutes are fairer but put quantities like "2.35 hours" on a customer's invoice.

Daily allowance is paid on top of salary, always

Always paid

Every driver earns the daily allowance for each day of duty, whether they're on a monthly salary or daily wages. One rule, no exceptions to track.

The alternative Pay it only to drivers who aren't on a monthly salary, treating the salary as already covering it.

Extra duty is billed only if you price it

Your choice

The driver is always paid for a second or third duty in a day. Whether the customer sees it depends on whether you added extra duty to that duty type — which is the same rule as every other allowance. Worth flagging because it's the one where a customer could reasonably object: their trip didn't cause the driver's earlier one.

The alternative Lock it to driver-side only, so it can never appear on an invoice by accident.

Off day is read from the driver's weekly off

From the roster

If a duty starts on the weekday recorded as that driver's off day, the allowance applies. It's taken from the driver's record rather than the attendance sheet, because attendance only marks present or absent — it has no way to say "this was their day off and they worked anyway".

The alternative Add an explicit off-day marker to the attendance sheet, so a one-off swapped rest day is handled correctly. More accurate, more daily upkeep.

Overtime is measured against the duty's scheduled timings

Needs your call

Overtime and early start need a baseline to measure against. Our recommendation is the duty's own scheduled reporting and drop times, because those are already recorded on every booking and it works from day one.

The alternative Measure against each driver's shift timings instead — truer to "designated hours", but those shift start and end times are currently blank for every driver on the system and would need filling in before the allowance would pay anything. The setting can be switched later, so this isn't a one-way door.

Where you'll see it

On the invoice

Allowances add into each booking's amount, so the totals and tax work exactly as they do today. The printed invoice breaks them out underneath each booking, line by line with quantity and rate, so the customer can see precisely what they're being charged for. Tax applies the same way it does to car hire.

In payroll

The allowances column becomes a real monthly total rather than a figure someone types in, built from every duty that driver ran. Opening it shows the breakdown: which allowance, from which duty, on which date.

Safeguards

Rates lock at calculation

The rate in force when a duty is calculated is stored with that duty. Raising your rates next month can never quietly rewrite an invoice you've already sent.

Your team can override

Every calculated amount stays editable on the duty slip up until the duty is billed. The system proposes; a person confirms.

Billed means frozen

Once a duty has been billed, its allowances stop recalculating altogether. Historical invoices stay exactly as they were issued.
