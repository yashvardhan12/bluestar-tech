Close Duty

The operator's path to finishing a duty the driver could not — because twenty-seven of thirty-one drivers have no app, and a duty that never closes never bills for the hours or the kilometres it actually ran.

The problem

Four columns, one writer, and most drivers can't reach it

Everything that turns a duty from scheduled into settled is written by exactly one function — driver_close_duty, gated on current_driver_id(). There is no operator equivalent. Nothing else in the product writes these:

CLOSED_AT when the duty actually ended — drives every overtime calculation
END_ODO closing reading — with start_odo, the entire extra-kilometre charge
END_ODO_PHOTO evidence behind the reading
SIGNATURE_PATH the passenger's authority, per FR-57

27 of 31 drivers have no auth_user_id. They cannot sign in, so they cannot reach that function. For 87% of the fleet the driver-app close is not a path that sometimes fails — it is a path that does not exist. Operator close is the primary capture route, and it has never been built.

The cost is quiet, which is what makes it expensive. computeAllowances returns zero overtime when closed_at is null. kmTotals and timeTotals return null without their pairs, so the duty slip prints an em-dash and the invoice goes out at base rate. Nobody sees an error; the operator sees a duty marked Completed and moves on.

The reframe

This is transcription, not review

“If the driver fails to add certain things while completing his duty, or rather does not even start the duty, the fallback option is for the operator to get all the details from him and add those himself and close the duty.”

That sentence is the whole specification. The operator is not auditing a completed record — they are on the phone to a driver, or reading a paper slip, writing down what they are told. Three consequences follow, and they shape everything below:

Field order follows the story, not the schema. A driver narrates a trip as left at seven, meter said forty-five thousand, dropped at seven in the evening, forty-five two sixty-two, two hundred rupees of toll. The form asks in that order.

The driver's phone number belongs on this screen. They are being called right now, or are about to be. Making the operator leave the modal to find a number is the single most likely reason this form gets abandoned half-filled.

Photographs and signatures cannot be obtained over a phone call. Their absence is the expected condition, not a failure state — and it is precisely what marks the record as operator-sourced. No extra flag is needed: end_odo_photo IS NULL AND closed_by_profile IS NOT NULL says it exactly.

Use cases

What the operator finds when they open a duty

The driver app writes its five fields at three separate moments, so the operator can arrive at any point in a partially-written record. Every combination below is reachable in production today. The form has to read the row and decide what to ask for — it can never assume it is starting from nothing, and it can never assume it is finishing someone else's work.

StateWhat existsHow it happensWhat Close Duty asks for
S0 · Blank Nothing. All five null. Driver has no login — the default for 27 of 31. Or has one and never opened it. Everything. Start pair, close pair, expenses. The common case by volume.
S1 · Started started_at, start_odo, photo Driver started, then stopped — battery, signal, or simply forgot. Two such duties live in production now. The close half only. The start block is the driver's and shows locked.
S2 · On board S1 + picked_up_at Driver confirmed pickup, never closed. Identical to S1. Pickup changes nothing about what is missing.
S3 · Closed All five, plus signature The driver app worked exactly as designed. Nothing — the action is absent. Changing a figure here is FR-56 correction, on the duty slip.
S4 · No-show no_show_reason, closed_at Driver marked a no-show, which closes the duty without an end reading. Nothing — already closed. The absent odometer is correct, not missing.
S5 · Back-dated Nothing, and the dates are already past Operator entered historical work, then allotted a vehicle to it. Same as S0. Different intent — a paper slip from weeks ago, not a call tonight.
S6 · No driver Vehicle allotted, driver_id null Allotment permits a vehicle with no driver. Someone still drove it. Same as S0, minus the person to call. Attribution names no driver.
S7 · Expenses only Expense rows, no odometer Driver logged tolls in the app but never closed the duty. S0's questions, with the logged expenses shown so they are not entered twice.

S3 and S4 are the two states where Close Duty must not appear at all. Both are already closed. Offering the action there would invite an operator to overwrite a signed record with a remembered one — and FR-57 makes the passenger signature the authority, so that trade is never worth making.

  • 1

    The driver's number is a tappable chip, not a lookup. This screen exists because a phone call is happening. Every second the operator spends leaving the modal to find a number is a second the form sits half-filled — and a half-filled close is worse than none, because the operator believes it is done. On S6 (no driver assigned) this row reads Not assigned and the chip is absent.

  • 2

    The banner names the state and its cause. Not “no data found” — Ramesh has no app login. The operator learns, once, why this duty needed them, and stops wondering whether the driver is about to submit something. On S1 this banner changes to name what the driver did record.

  • 3

    Times default to the planned window, and say so. Never now(). There is a duty in production due to drop on 25 July that was closed on 17 August, which computes to roughly five hundred and fifty hours of overtime. Defaulting to the booking's own window makes overtime resolve to exactly zero — the neutral, defensible answer. Overtime can only ever be created by an operator deliberately typing a later time, which is exactly the deliberation it deserves. The from the booking hint is what stops a default being mistaken for a reading.

  • 4

    “Not known” is a checkbox, because an empty field is ambiguous. FR-59 explicitly permits closing with no readings, and FR-65 makes such a duty invoice at base rate. An empty input cannot distinguish haven't typed it yet from the driver does not have it — the first is an unfinished form, the second is a recorded fact. Ticking the box greys the input and switches the panel at 5 to the base-rate statement.

  • 5

    The consequence panel is a sanity check, not a dashboard. It recomputes as the operator types, so a mistyped digit is caught while the driver is still on the line — “142 kilometres, does that sound right?” is a question worth asking before saving, and impossible to ask afterwards. It shows what these numbers do, never what they are worth in rupees; pricing belongs on the duty slip and the invoice, and putting money here would turn a transcription screen into a negotiation.

  • 6

    Provenance is stated in plain words, once. No badge, no icon, no tooltip. The operator is about to create a record with less evidence behind it than a driver close, and should know that as they press the button. Structurally it is already true — closed_by_profile set with end_odo_photo null is an operator close and nothing else — so this sentence describes reality rather than asking for a new column.

  • 7

    The driver's half is a different material entirely. Grey ground, no input borders, its own header naming the source and the time it was captured. It cannot be typed into. Changing one of these figures is FR-56 correction — a separate, attributed action that writes corrected_by and corrected_at, reached through a deliberate Correct link rather than by clicking into a field. If closing and overriding-the-driver were the same gesture, every operator close would silently become a potential correction, and the signature would stop being the authority FR-57 says it is. The evidence chip sits beside the figure it belongs to, so a disputed reading is one click from the photograph that settles it. Note the pickup row carries no Correct — it is an observed event with no billing consequence, so there is nothing to correct it to.

  • 8

    The backwards reading is caught here, not by Postgres. The duties_odo_forward constraint would reject this row anyway, but a constraint violation arrives as an opaque failure after a round trip, with the driver still on the phone and the operator unsure what to retype. Inline, against the start reading that is already on screen, it is self-explanatory — and naming the number it must exceed means the operator can ask the right follow-up question immediately. The submit button disables while the field is bad; the consequence panel shows an em-dash for distance rather than a negative number, because a wrong figure must never render as a plausible one.

State 3 · the escape hatch

He doesn't remember the readings

FR-59 is explicit that this must be possible: “close without them if they do not [have the readings]. A duty closed with no readings invoices at base rate per FR-65.” It is not a degraded save to be discouraged — it is the correct outcome when the information is genuinely gone, and it must be as easy to reach as any other close. Discouraging it produces something far worse than a base-rate invoice: an invented odometer reading.

    Ticking the box empties and disables the field rather than hiding it. Field anatomy

Where every default comes from

A default that cannot be traced is a guess wearing a uniform. Each of these is derived from something already on the duty, and each is labelled on screen so the operator knows they are looking at an assumption rather than a reading.

FieldDefaultWhy that, and not something else
started_at start_date + reporting_time The time the duty was booked to begin. Wrong by minutes at worst; now() is wrong by days.
closed_at end_date + est_drop_time Makes overtime resolve to exactly zero — the neutral result. Overtime becomes billable only when an operator types a later time on purpose.
start_odo Empty There is nothing on the duty to infer a reading from. A guessed odometer is worse than no odometer, because it looks authoritative.
end_odo Empty Same. Validated against start_odo the moment both are present.
status 'Completed' Written on save unless already Billed or Cancelled, matching what driver_close_duty does.
closed_by_profile auth.uid() Column has existed unused since migration 025. With a null end_odo_photo it is what identifies an operator close.
corrected_at now() The one place now() is right: it records when the entry was made, not when the duty ran.

Two calls follow every successful save, both of which already exist: syncBookingStatus(bookingId) to roll the parent booking up, and syncDutyAllowances(dutyId) to recompute allowance lines. The second is easy to miss — those lines were computed while closed_at was null, so every overtime quantity is sitting at zero until something re-syncs them.

Refusals & edges

When the action is absent, and when it argues back

Action absent

Already closed (S3, S4). closed_at is not null. Close Duty does not appear in the menu at all. Correction lives on the duty slip, where the evidence is.

Action absent

Billed or Cancelled. A billed duty's figures are on an invoice someone has been sent. Cancelled duties never ran.

Inline notice

The driver could still close this. Duty's driver has a login and now falls inside the duty window. A notice names them; the close is still permitted. FR-59's gate was dropped deliberately — this is the mitigation.

Blocks save

End reading not above start. Named inline against the start figure. Mirrors duties_odo_forward so the database never has to be the one to say no.

Blocks save

Dropped before it left. closed_at earlier than started_at. Common when a multi-day duty's end date is mistyped.

Warns, allows

Dates in the past. Entirely legitimate — back-dated and historical entry is a named use case (S5). No guard, no warning. This is the point.

Reload & tell

The driver closed it first. Server-side coalesce means the driver's figures win, which is correct — they had the odometer in front of them. The modal reloads and shows what was submitted rather than reporting a failure.

Adapts

No driver assigned (S6). Driver row reads Not assigned, no phone chip, no name in the banner. Everything else is unchanged — the vehicle still ran.

Decisions carried

Settled here, and deferred

Settled

FR-59's time gate is dropped

The PRD blocks operator close until a duty's scheduled end has passed, on the principle that “an in-progress duty belongs to the driver”. That assumed drivers use the app. For twenty-seven of thirty-one, the duty belongs to no driver in any operational sense, and the gate would make the operator wait on duties nobody else could ever close. Close Duty is available on any On-Going duty. The risk it opens — closing under a driver mid-trip — is answered with the inline notice above rather than a block.

Settled

No new RPC, no migration

Operator RLS on duties is a single ALL policy scoped through the booking. The forward-odometer constraint is already in place. All three audit columns have existed since migration 025. This is a plain .update() from the client — the same call the page already makes to change a driver or cancel a duty.

Ships together

FR-58 — the “Needs closing” list

Not optional alongside this modal; it is the other half of it. The addendum is explicit that FR-59 removes the only existing mechanism for closing a forgotten duty, so without FR-58's list “duties will silently stop being invoiced”. One filter on the duties view — effective_status IN ('Completed','On-Going') AND closed_at IS NULL — catches a stuck duty and a back-dated allotment with the same rule. Without it nobody finds these rows, because they already read as Completed.

Unresolved

FR-54 is violated in production

FR-54 requires that a duty is “not auto-Completed because its end date passed if the driver never closed it”. Migration 026's duties_status.effective_status, written after the PRD, does exactly that — which is why the duties most in need of closing are the ones that look finished. Whether the view changes or FR-54 retires is an architecture decision, not a UX one. Flagged, not answered.

Out of scope

Bulk close, and FR-56 correction

A day's duties closed from one grid is a real need at twenty-seven drivers, but speculative until this modal has been used and found slow. Correcting an already-closed duty is FR-56 and belongs on the duty slip beside the evidence, not here. Both are deliberately absent.
